01 — The Field

Early Life

Imran Khan Niazi was born on 5 October 1952 in Lahore, into a Pashtun family of the Niazi clan. His father, Ikramullah Khan Niazi, was a civil engineer; his mother, Shaukat Khanum, came from a Sufi family in Mianwali. He grew up as the only brother among four sisters.

He was enrolled at Lahore's Cathedral School and later Aitchison College, before moving to the United Kingdom to attend the Royal Grammar School Worcester. In 1972 he joined Keble College, Oxford, to read Philosophy, Politics and Economics, graduating in 1975. His years at Oxford shaped his outlook on governance and global affairs, laying the groundwork for the two very different careers that followed — first on the cricket field, then in national politics.

02 — The Field

Cricket Journey

Khan made his international debut for Pakistan in 1971 against England, aged 18, initially as a medium-fast bowler. Over the following years he developed into one of the sport's most lethal fast bowlers, becoming known for pioneering reverse swing alongside a fierce bouncer. He was appointed national captain in 1982, restructured team selection around merit, and led Pakistan through its first-ever Test series wins in India and England.

On 25 March 1992, at age 39, he led Pakistan to victory over England in the Cricket World Cup final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground — famously rallying his side as the "Cornered Tigers" — the country's only World Cup title to date.

03 — Beyond the Boundary

Philanthropy

In 1994, Khan founded the Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital & Research Centre in Lahore, named after his mother, who died of cancer. Built substantially through public donations, it became Pakistan's first dedicated cancer treatment and research facility, offering free treatment to a majority of patients unable to pay. A second campus was later opened in Peshawar, followed by further expansion. He also established Namal College in Mianwali, a university built in partnership with the University of Bradford.

04 — The Second Innings

Entering Politics

Khan founded the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) on 25 April 1996. For its first fifteen years the party struggled electorally. A large rally at Minar-e-Pakistan, Lahore, on 30 October 2011 marked a turning point in PTI's public support, and in the 2013 general election the party formed a provincial government in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. By the 2018 general election, PTI had become the largest party in the National Assembly, and Khan was sworn in as Prime Minister on 18 August 2018.

His premiership coincided with significant economic strain, including rising inflation, and was marked by an evolving relationship with Pakistan's powerful military establishment — historically a decisive factor in the country's civilian governments.

05 — Innings Break

Removal and Aftermath

Apr 2022
Political

Removed as Prime Minister

Khan was ousted via a no-confidence vote in the National Assembly, becoming the first Pakistani prime minister removed this way. He alleged the vote was the result of foreign interference, a claim the United States denied.

Nov 2022
Incident

Assassination attempt

Khan was shot and wounded in the leg during a party rally in Wazirabad while leading a protest march toward Islamabad demanding early elections.

May 2023
Legal

First arrest and unrest

Khan's arrest on 9 May 2023 on corruption charges triggered nationwide protests and clashes; he was released two days later after the Supreme Court ruled the arrest unlawful.

Aug 2023
Legal

Convicted and jailed

Khan was arrested again in August 2023, this time in connection with the Toshakhana case involving undeclared state gifts. He has remained in custody since, held at Adiala Jail in Rawalpindi.

2024
Legal

Further convictions

Additional convictions followed, including in the Cipher case concerning leaked state secrets, and in cases connected to the May 2023 riots. Some sentences have since been suspended or overturned on appeal, while others remain in effect. PTI disputed the outcome of the February 2024 general election, alleging rigging; the party's candidates, running as independents after being barred from using the PTI symbol, still won the largest single bloc of seats.

2025–26
Ongoing

Continued detention and health concerns

Khan's legal team and PTI officials have reported he is held largely in solitary confinement with restricted family access — characterisations the government disputes. In early 2026, his lawyers told Pakistan's Supreme Court he had lost significant vision in one eye due to a retinal condition his team attributes to delayed medical care. A UN special rapporteur raised concerns about his detention conditions in December 2025.

07 — Current Over

Where Things Stand

As of mid-2026, Khan remains imprisoned at Adiala Jail, facing well over a hundred cases across Pakistan, while continuing to lead PTI in opposition. His legal team is pursuing court appeals, including in the Al-Qadir Trust case, alongside medical-grounds petitions related to his eye condition.

To supporters, a wrongfully persecuted leader; to critics, a politician facing legitimate legal accountability — few figures in the region draw a more polarised response.

Analysts have suggested that any resolution may ultimately depend less on individual court rulings than on a broader political settlement between PTI and Pakistan's military establishment.
